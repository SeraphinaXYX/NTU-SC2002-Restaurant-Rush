\section{Project overview}
The manager runs a restaurant for all 18 turns, deciding seating, ordering,
serving and kitchen priorities under limited staff capacity. The required base
setting has two tables and six arrivals, on turns 1, 4, 7, 10, 13 and 16.
Victory requires at least four paid customers, revenue of at least \$45.00,
and average paid-customer satisfaction of at least 60. If nobody has paid,
the display shows N/A rather than a misleading average of zero.

The game was built in three stages. Stage~1 implements the complete base game
with regular customers. Stage~2 adds VIP and Critic customer types via
polymorphism. Stage~3 introduces Host seating, ComboMeal composition and
Happy Hour pricing. Each stage is tagged and independently runnable.

\subsection{Rules and reproducibility}
Money is stored as integer cents. Regular customers lose 8 satisfaction per
unserved waiting turn, VIPs lose 5 and Critics lose 12. Arrival~2 is a VIP and
arrival~3 a Critic in Stages~2 and~3. The manager never chooses the food.
Regular/VIP choices use arrival ID modulo the menu size; Critics select the
item with most preparation units, then highest price. Stage~1 cycles Salad,
Burger and Pasta; Stage~3 adds Salad + Pasta as the fourth choice.

The Waiter acts before the Chef. A dish finished this turn can be served next
turn at the earliest. A customer served on turn $t$ becomes ready to pay on
$t+1$ and can pay on $t+2$ at the earliest. Tables are released only at payment
or abandonment, and unpaid orders contribute no revenue. The turn-16 arrival
cannot complete payment before closing. All mandatory features run offline.

Stage~3 adds a Host with one seating action, in addition to the Waiter;
a composed Salad + Pasta combo costing \$24.30 with three preparation units;
and a once-per-game Happy Hour lasting its activation turn and the next.
Happy Hour restores up to 15 satisfaction at turn start to waiting/unserved
customers and discounts new orders by 20\%, after the VIP discount.
The bill is fixed when ordering. Repeated input and the same source revision
determine the run; all test fixtures are fully reproducible.
